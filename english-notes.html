⏳ Tenses

Present Tense

1. Simple Present

Used for habits, routines, general truths and repeated actions.

Structure: Subject + V1 / V1+s/es + Object
Example: She goes to school every day.

2. Present Continuous

Used for an action happening now or around the present time.

Structure: Subject + is/am/are + V-ing
Example: They are playing football.

3. Present Perfect

Used for an action completed recently or having a connection with the present.

Structure: Subject + has/have + V3
Example: I have finished my homework.

4. Present Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + has/have been + V-ing
Example: She has been studying for two hours.

Past Tense

1. Simple Past

Structure: Subject + V2
Example: He visited Delhi yesterday.

2. Past Continuous

Structure: Subject + was/were + V-ing
Example: They were watching television.

3. Past Perfect

Structure: Subject + had + V3
Example: She had left before I arrived.

4. Past Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + had been + V-ing
Example: He had been working for three hours.

Future Tense

1. Simple Future

Structure: Subject + will + V1
Example: I will help you.

2. Future Continuous

Structure: Subject + will be + V-ing
Example: I will be studying at 8 PM.

3. Future Perfect

Structure: Subject + will have + V3
Example: She will have completed the work by Monday.

4. Future Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + will have been + V-ing
Example: By June, he will have been working here for two years.

🔧 Modals

Common Modals

Modal Common Use Example
Can Ability / Permission I can swim.
Could Past ability / Polite request Could you help me?
May Permission / Possibility May I come in?
Might Weak possibility It might rain.
Must Necessity / Strong obligation You must obey the rules.
Should Advice / Duty You should study regularly.
Would Polite request / Preference Would you like tea?
Important: A modal is generally followed by the base form of the verb.
Correct: She can swim.
Incorrect: She can swims.

📌 Determiners

What are Determiners?

Determiners are words placed before nouns to give information about them.

Examples: a book, the boy, some water, many students, my bag

Important Determiners

  • Some: Usually used with plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns in affirmative contexts.
  • Any: Commonly used in questions and negative sentences.
  • Much: Used with uncountable nouns.
  • Many: Used with plural countable nouns.
  • Few: Used with plural countable nouns.
  • Little: Used with uncountable nouns.
  • Each: Refers to members individually.
  • Every: Refers to all members individually within a group.

👥 Subject-Verb Agreement

Basic Rule

The verb should agree with its subject in number and person.

Correct: He plays cricket.
Correct: They play cricket.

Important Rules

  • Singular subject generally takes a singular verb.
  • Plural subject generally takes a plural verb.
  • Words such as each, every, everyone, everybody generally take singular verbs.
  • With neither...nor and either...or, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject.
Example: Neither the teacher nor the students are ready.

🔄 Reported Speech

Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct speech gives the exact words of the speaker. Indirect speech reports the meaning without quoting the exact words.

Direct: He said, "I am tired."

Indirect: He said that he was tired.

Common Changes

Direct Common Reported Form
am / is was
are were
has / have had
will would
can could
may might
Remember: Tense changes depend on the reporting verb, time reference and context. Do not change a tense mechanically in every sentence.

🔀 Active & Passive Voice

Active Voice

The subject performs the action.

Active: The teacher praised Rahul.

Passive Voice

The subject receives the action.

Passive: Rahul was praised by the teacher.
Basic idea: In passive voice, the object of the active sentence generally becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

Important

  • The main verb changes to an appropriate form of be + past participle (V3).
  • The tense of the original sentence must be preserved.
  • The agent may be introduced with by when necessary.

🔗 Conjunctions

What are Conjunctions?

Conjunctions connect words, phrases or clauses.

Common Types

  • Coordinating: and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor
  • Subordinating: because, although, if, when, while, unless
  • Correlative: either...or, neither...nor, both...and
Example: I wanted to go, but it was raining.

🧩 Clauses

What is a Clause?

A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a finite verb.

Independent Clause

Can stand alone as a complete sentence.

Example: I went home.

Dependent Clause

Cannot normally stand alone and depends on another clause.

Example: because I was tired
Combined: I went home because I was tired.

📖 Articles

A, An and The

A

Used before a singular countable noun beginning with a consonant sound.

a book, a university

An

Used before a singular countable noun beginning with a vowel sound.

an apple, an hour

The

Used when referring to a specific or already identified noun.

The book on the table is mine.
Sound matters: We say a university because university begins with a /y/ sound, while an hour begins with a vowel sound.

✏️ Editing & Omission

Editing

Editing questions require you to identify and correct grammatical errors in a sentence or passage.

Wrong: She go to school every day.
Correct: She goes to school every day.

Omission

In omission questions, a word is missing from a sentence. You have to identify the missing word and place it correctly.

Example: He is good mathematics.
Missing word: at
Correct: He is good at mathematics.

⚡ Quick Revision

Tenses

Check the time of the action first, then choose the appropriate tense.

Modals

Modal + base form of verb.

Determiners

Check whether the noun is countable, uncountable, singular or plural.

Agreement

Match the verb with the actual subject.

Reported Speech

Check pronouns, tense, time words and sentence type.

Voice

Identify the object and preserve the original tense.

Prepositions

Learn common combinations through examples.

Articles

Focus on the sound, not just the spelling.
