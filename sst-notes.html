📚 SST Important Notes

Class 9 Social Science — Quick Revision & Exam-Focused Notes

🏛️ History

1. The French Revolution

Important Points

  • French Revolution began in 1789.
  • French society was divided into three estates.
  • First Estate → Clergy.
  • Second Estate → Nobility.
  • Third Estate → Common people.
  • The Bastille was stormed on 14 July 1789.
  • The Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen promoted liberty and equality.
  • France became a republic in 1792.
  • Louis XVI was executed in 1793.
Key Idea: Liberty, equality and fraternity became important revolutionary ideals.

2. Socialism in Europe and the Russian Revolution

  • Socialists criticised the inequalities created by industrial capitalism.
  • Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels developed important socialist ideas.
  • The Russian Revolution took place in 1917.
  • The February Revolution led to the fall of Tsar Nicholas II.
  • The Bolsheviks, led by Vladimir Lenin, came to power in the October Revolution.
  • Russia later became part of the Soviet Union.
Remember: 1917 → Russian Revolution.

3. Nazism and the Rise of Hitler

  • Germany faced severe economic and political problems after World War I.
  • The Treaty of Versailles placed major restrictions on Germany.
  • Adolf Hitler became Chancellor of Germany in 1933.
  • The Nazi regime established a dictatorship.
  • Nazism promoted extreme racial ideology and persecution.
  • Jews and several other groups were persecuted by the Nazi regime.
Key Idea: Nazi Germany was a dictatorship based on racial ideology, militarism and extreme nationalism.

4. Forest Society and Colonialism

  • Colonial governments introduced scientific forestry to control forests.
  • Large areas were converted into commercial plantations.
  • Forest communities depended on forests for food, fuel and livelihood.
  • Colonial forest laws restricted traditional forest use.
  • People resisted these restrictions in different regions.

🌍 Geography

1. India — Size and Location

  • India lies entirely in the Northern Hemisphere.
  • India extends between approximately 8°4'N and 37°6'N latitudes.
  • India extends between approximately 68°7'E and 97°25'E longitudes.
  • The Tropic of Cancer passes through India.
  • India has a central location in the Indian Ocean.
Important: India's Standard Meridian is 82°30'E.

2. Physical Features of India

Major Physiographic Divisions

  • The Himalayan Mountains
  • The Northern Plains
  • The Peninsular Plateau
  • The Indian Desert
  • The Coastal Plains
  • The Islands
Himalayas: Young fold mountains forming India's northern mountain system.

3. Drainage

  • Drainage refers to the river system of an area.
  • Himalayan rivers are generally perennial.
  • Peninsular rivers are generally seasonal.
  • Major Himalayan river systems include the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra.
  • Important Peninsular rivers include the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.

4. Climate

  • India has a monsoon type of climate.
  • The monsoon strongly influences rainfall and temperature.
  • Major seasons include cold weather, hot weather, advancing monsoon and retreating monsoon.
  • Rainfall is unevenly distributed across India.
Key Term: Monsoon refers to the seasonal reversal of wind patterns.

5. Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

  • Natural vegetation depends on temperature, rainfall and soil.
  • Major vegetation types include tropical evergreen, tropical deciduous, thorn forests, mountain vegetation and mangrove forests.
  • Wildlife conservation is important for maintaining biodiversity.

🏛️ Civics

1. What is Democracy?

Democracy: A form of government in which rulers are elected by the people.

Key Features

  • Final decision-making power should rest with elected representatives.
  • Free and fair elections should take place regularly.
  • Each adult citizen has equal voting rights.
  • Citizens should have basic rights and freedoms.
  • Government should work according to constitutional rules.

2. Constitutional Design

  • A constitution contains the basic rules and principles of a country.
  • It defines powers of government institutions.
  • It protects citizens' rights.
  • The Indian Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949.
  • It came into effect on 26 January 1950.
Remember: 26 November → Constitution adopted
26 January → Constitution came into force

3. Electoral Politics

  • Elections allow citizens to choose their representatives.
  • India follows universal adult franchise.
  • Citizens aged 18 years and above can vote, subject to legal eligibility.
  • Constituencies are geographical areas represented by elected members.
  • The Election Commission conducts elections in India.

4. Working of Institutions

  • Parliament is the supreme law-making body at the Union level.
  • The executive implements laws and runs administration.
  • The judiciary interprets laws and settles disputes.
  • The President is the constitutional head of the Union.
  • The Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise executive power in the parliamentary system.

💰 Economics

1. The Story of Village Palampur

  • Farming is the main production activity discussed in Palampur.
  • Other activities include small-scale manufacturing, transport, shopkeeping and dairy.
  • Factors of production include land, labour, physical capital and human capital.
  • Multiple cropping means growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during a year.

2. People as Resource

  • Population becomes a resource when people develop their skills and knowledge.
  • Education and health improve the quality of human resources.
  • Human capital refers to the skills and knowledge embodied in people.
  • Unemployment means a situation in which people willing and able to work do not get suitable employment.

3. Poverty as a Challenge

  • Poverty is not limited to low income; it can also involve deprivation of basic needs.
  • Education, health, housing and adequate food are important dimensions of well-being.
  • Poverty is measured using different indicators and methods.
  • Government programmes aim to reduce poverty and improve living conditions.

4. Food Security in India

  • Food security means availability, accessibility and affordability of food.
  • The government maintains buffer stocks of foodgrains.
  • The Public Distribution System helps provide foodgrains to eligible households.
  • The Food Corporation of India plays an important role in procurement and storage.
Food Security: Ensuring that all people have sufficient, safe and nutritious food when needed.

📌 Important Definitions

  • Democracy: Government by elected representatives chosen by the people.
  • Constitution: A set of basic rules and principles according to which a country is governed.
  • Monsoon: Seasonal reversal of wind direction.
  • Drainage: The river system of an area.
  • Human Capital: Skills and knowledge possessed by people.
  • Food Security: Availability, accessibility and affordability of food.
  • Poverty: A condition involving inadequate resources to meet basic needs and achieve an acceptable standard of living.
  • Multiple Cropping: Growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during a year.
