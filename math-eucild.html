📚 Introduction

Euclid was an ancient Greek mathematician whose work formed the foundation of classical geometry.

Euclid organised geometry using definitions, axioms, postulates, propositions and logical proofs.

Definition: A statement that explains the meaning of a mathematical term.
Axiom: A statement accepted as true without proof and used generally in mathematics.
Postulate: An assumption accepted without proof that is specifically related to geometry.
Theorem: A mathematical statement that is proved using definitions, axioms, postulates and earlier results.

📐 Euclid's Five Postulates

1. A straight line may be drawn from any one point to any other point.
2. A finite straight line can be extended continuously in a straight line.
3. A circle can be drawn with any centre and any radius.
4. All right angles are equal to one another.
5. If a straight line falling on two straight lines makes the interior angles on the same side sum to less than two right angles, the two lines, if extended indefinitely, meet on that side.

🧠 Important Axioms / Common Notions

• Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another.

• If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.

• If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.

• Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another.

• The whole is greater than the part.

✍️ Solved Examples

Example 1

If AB = CD and CD = EF, prove that AB = EF.

Since AB = CD and CD = EF,
AB = EF.

This follows from the axiom: Things equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
Example 2

If two equal quantities have the same quantity added to them, the resulting quantities remain equal.
Example 3

If a line segment is extended beyond one endpoint, the original segment remains part of the extended straight line.
