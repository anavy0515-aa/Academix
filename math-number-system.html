Number Systems

Complete Notes • Important Concepts • Examples • Quiz

📖 1. Introduction

A number system is a system used to represent numbers. In Class 9, we study different types of numbers such as natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, rational numbers and irrational numbers.

Types of Numbers

  • Natural Numbers
  • Whole Numbers
  • Integers
  • Rational Numbers
  • Irrational Numbers
  • Real Numbers

🔢 2. Types of Numbers

Natural Numbers (N)

Natural numbers are counting numbers.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}

Whole Numbers (W)

Whole numbers include zero along with natural numbers.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...}

Integers (Z)

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.

Z = {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}

Rational Numbers (Q)

A number that can be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0, is called a rational number.

p/q , where q ≠ 0
Examples:
1/2, 3/4, -5/7, 2, 0

Irrational Numbers

Numbers that cannot be expressed in the form p/q are called irrational numbers.

Examples:
√2, √3, √5, π

Real Numbers (R)

The collection of rational and irrational numbers is called the set of real numbers.

Real Numbers = Rational Numbers + Irrational Numbers

📏 3. Representation on Number Line

Every real number can be represented by a unique point on the number line.

Important:

Positive numbers lie to the right of zero, while negative numbers lie to the left of zero.

🔢 4. Decimal Expansions

Rational numbers can have terminating or non-terminating recurring decimal expansions.

Terminating Decimal

1/2 = 0.5
3/4 = 0.75
7/8 = 0.875

Non-Terminating Recurring Decimal

1/3 = 0.3333...
2/11 = 0.181818...

Irrational Decimal

Irrational numbers have non-terminating and non-repeating decimal expansions.

√2 = 1.41421356...
π = 3.14159265...

⭐ 5. Important Result

Let p/q be a rational number in its lowest form, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

The decimal expansion of p/q terminates if and only if the prime factorisation of q is of the form:

q = 2ⁿ × 5ᵐ

where n and m are non-negative integers.

Example:

3/40

40 = 2³ × 5

Therefore, 3/40 has a terminating decimal expansion.

⚡ 6. Laws of Exponents

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
a⁰ = 1
a⁻ᵐ = 1/aᵐ
aᵐ⁄ⁿ = ⁿ√(aᵐ)

⭐ 7. Important Points for Exam

  • Every natural number is a whole number.
  • Every whole number is an integer.
  • Every integer is a rational number.
  • Every rational number is a real number.
  • Irrational numbers are also real numbers.
  • √2, √3 and √5 are irrational numbers.
  • Every rational number has either a terminating or recurring decimal expansion.
  • Irrational numbers have non-terminating and non-repeating decimal expansions.
  • 0 is a whole number, integer and rational number.
