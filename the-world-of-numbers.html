The World of Numbers

Class 9 Mathematics · Chapter 3 · Exercise-wise Solutions

Quick Revision

Natural numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, …

Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, …

Integers: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …

Rational number: \(p/q\), where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Irrational number: A real number that cannot be expressed as \(p/q\).

Terminating decimal: In lowest form, the denominator has only prime factors 2 and/or 5.

Pythagoras theorem: Hypotenuse² = Side₁² + Side₂².

Exercise Set 3.1 — Natural Numbers

Question 1

A merchant exchanges bags of spices for copper ingots, receiving 15 ingots for every 2 bags. How many ingots does he receive for 12 bags?

Show Solution
For 2 bags, ingots = 15.
For 12 bags: \(12\div2=6\) groups.
Total ingots = \(6\times15=\boxed{90}\).
Question 2

Explore the sequence of prime numbers and explain what makes a number prime.

Show Solution
A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. Examples include 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 and 13. Remember that 1 is not prime.
Question 3

Are natural numbers closed under subtraction?

Show Solution
No. For example, \(3-5=-2\), which is not a natural number. Therefore, natural numbers are not closed under subtraction.
Question 4

Explain how counting finger joints can be connected with the number 12.

Show Solution
Each of four fingers has three joints. So the count is \(4\times3=12\). This illustrates why groups of twelve have been useful in counting systems.

Exercise Set 3.2 — Integers

Practice 1

A temperature is 4°C and then falls by 15°C. Find the new temperature.

Show Solution
New temperature = \(4-15=\boxed{-11^\circ C}\).
Practice 2

A person owes ₹850, earns ₹1,200 and then loses ₹450. Find the net balance.

Show Solution
Net balance \(=-850+1200-450\)
\(=350-450=\boxed{-100}\).
The person still owes ₹100.
Practice 3

Calculate: (i) \((-12)\times5\), (ii) \((-8)\times(-7)\), (iii) \(0-(-14)\), (iv) \((-20)\div4\).

Show Solution
(i) −60
(ii) 56
(iii) 14
(iv) −5
Practice 4

Simplify \(10-(-5)\).

Show Solution
Subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its positive value: \(10-(-5)=10+5=\boxed{15}\).

Exercise Set 3.3 — Rational Numbers

Question 1

Show that the following pairs are equal: (i) 2/3 and 4/6, (ii) 5/4 and 10/8, (iii) −3/5 and −6/10, (iv) 9/3 and 3.

Show Solution
(i) \(4/6=2/3\)
(ii) \(10/8=5/4\)
(iii) \(-6/10=-3/5\)
(iv) \(9/3=3\)
Each pair represents equal rational numbers.
Question 2

Find: (i) 2/5 + 3/10, (ii) 7/12 + 5/8, (iii) −4/7 + 3/14.

Show Solution
(i) \(4/10+3/10=\boxed{7/10}\)
(ii) \(14/24+15/24=\boxed{29/24}\)
(iii) \(-8/14+3/14=\boxed{-5/14}\)
Question 3

Find: (i) 5/6 − 1/4, (ii) 11/8 − 3/4, (iii) −7/9 − (−2/3).

Show Solution
(i) \(10/12-3/12=\boxed{7/12}\)
(ii) \(11/8-6/8=\boxed{5/8}\)
(iii) \(-7/9+6/9=\boxed{-1/9}\)
Question 4

Multiply: (i) 2/3 × 3/10, (ii) 7/11 × 5/8, (iii) −4/7 × 5/14.

Show Solution
(i) \(6/30=\boxed{1/5}\)
(ii) \(\boxed{35/88}\)
(iii) \(-20/98=\boxed{-10/49}\)
Question 5

Divide: (i) 2/3 ÷ 3/10, (ii) 7/11 ÷ 5/8, (iii) −4/7 ÷ 5/14.

Show Solution
Divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal.
(i) \(2/3\times10/3=\boxed{20/9}\)
(ii) \(7/11\times8/5=\boxed{56/55}\)
(iii) \(-4/7\times14/5=\boxed{-8/5}\)
Question 6

Verify the distributive property for \((1/2+3/4)\times8/3\).

Show Solution
LHS \(=(2/4+3/4)\times8/3=5/4\times8/3=10/3\).
RHS \(=1/2\times8/3+3/4\times8/3=4/3+2=10/3\).
Hence LHS = RHS.
Question 7

Simplify \(\frac79(\frac67-\frac34)\).

Show Solution
\(=\frac23-\frac7{12}\)
\(=\frac8{12}-\frac7{12}=\boxed{\frac1{12}}\).
Question 8

Verify \(\frac56(x+\frac35)=\frac56x+\frac12\).

Show Solution
Expanding the left-hand side: \[ \frac56x+\frac56\times\frac35 =\frac56x+\frac12 \] This equals the right-hand side for every rational value of \(x\).

Exercise Set 3.4 — Rational Numbers on the Number Line

Practice 1

Represent −5/4, 2/3 and 3/2 on a number line.

Show Solution and Figure
Their approximate positions are −1.25, 0.667 and 1.5, respectively.
Practice 2

Find a rational number between −1/4 and 5/12.

Show Solution
\(-1/4=-3/12\). The number \(1/6=2/12\) lies between \(-3/12\) and \(5/12\).
Practice 3

A tailor has 15¾ metres of cloth. Each garment needs 2¼ metres. How many complete garments can be made?

Show Solution
\(15\frac34=63/4\), and \(2\frac14=9/4\).
Number \(=(63/4)\div(9/4)=63/9=\boxed7\).
Practice 4

Find three rational numbers between 3.1415 and 3.1416.

Show Solution
Examples: 3.14151, 3.14153 and 3.14157.
Practice 5

How can you find a rational number between any two distinct rational numbers \(a\) and \(b\)?

Show Solution
Their average \((a+b)/2\) lies strictly between them. If \(a<b\), then: \[ a<\frac{a+b}{2}<b \]

Exercise Set 3.5 — Decimal Representations

Practice 1

Decide whether 7/20, 4/15 and 13/250 have terminating decimal expansions.

Show Solution
(i) \(7/20=0.35\), terminating.
(ii) \(4/15=0.2666\ldots\), repeating.
(iii) \(13/250=0.052\), terminating.
A reduced fraction terminates if its denominator contains only prime factors 2 and/or 5.
Practice 2

Find the repeating block of 1/13.

Show Solution
\[ \frac1{13}=0.076923076923\ldots \] The repeating block is 076923.
Practice 3

Classify √81, √12 and 0.333… as rational or irrational.

Show Solution
(i) \(\sqrt{81}=9\), rational.
(ii) \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\), irrational.
(iii) \(0.333\ldots=1/3\), rational.
Practice 4

Show that 0.999… equals 1.

Show Solution
Let \(x=0.999\ldots\). Then \(10x=9.999\ldots\).
Subtracting gives \(9x=9\), so \(x=1\). Therefore \(0.999\ldots=1\).

Q1

Convert 3/50 and 2/9 into decimal expansions using long division.

Show Solution
\(3/50=0.06\), terminating.
\(2/9=0.222\ldots\), non-terminating repeating.
Q2

Prove that √5 is irrational.

Show Solution
Assume \(\sqrt5=p/q\) in lowest terms. Then \(p^2=5q^2\), so 5 divides \(p\). Write \(p=5k\). Substituting gives \(q^2=5k^2\), so 5 also divides \(q\). This contradicts the fraction being in lowest terms. Thus √5 is irrational.
Q3

Convert the following decimals into p/q form: 12.6, 0.0120, 3.052, 1.23535…, 0.232323…, 2.05555…, 2.125, 3.125 and 2.1625.

Show Solution
(i) \(12.6=63/5\)
(ii) \(0.0120=3/250\)
(iii) \(3.052=763/250\)
(iv) \(1.23535\ldots=1223/990\)
(v) \(0.232323\ldots=23/99\)
(vi) \(2.05555\ldots=37/18\)
(vii) \(2.125=17/8\)
(viii) \(3.125=25/8\)
(ix) \(2.1625=173/80\)
Q4

Locate 0.532 and 1.151515… on a number line.

Show Solution
\(0.532\) lies between 0.53 and 0.54.
\(1.151515\ldots=38/33\approx1.1515\), which lies between 1.15 and 1.16.
Q5

Find six rational numbers between 3 and 4.

Show Solution
Examples: \(25/8,26/8,27/8,28/8,29/8,30/8\). All are strictly between 3 and 4.
Q6

Find five rational numbers between 2/5 and 3/5.

Show Solution
Write \(2/5=12/30\), \(3/5=18/30\). Five choices are \(13/30,14/30,15/30,16/30,17/30\).
Q7

Find five rational numbers between 1/6 and 2/5.

Show Solution
\(1/6=5/30\), \(2/5=12/30\). Five examples: \(6/30,7/30,8/30,9/30,10/30\).
Q8

Solve \(x/3+5x/16=15\).

Show Solution
\(16x/48+15x/48=15\)
\(31x/48=15\)
\(\boxed{x=720/31}\).
Q9

If \(a+1/b=0\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are non-zero rational numbers, determine the sign of \(ab\).

Show Solution
Multiplying by \(b\): \(ab+1=0\), hence \(ab=-1\), which is negative.
Q10

Explain how a terminating decimal can be expressed as a fraction with a power of 10 as denominator.

Show Solution
If a decimal has four places, remove the decimal point and write the resulting integer over 10,000. Then simplify the fraction if needed. For example, \(0.1234=1234/10000=617/5000\).
Q11

Without long division, decide whether 18/125 terminates.

Show Solution
\(125=5^3\), so the decimal terminates. \(18/125=144/1000=\boxed{0.144}\).
Q12

How many decimal places can a fraction with denominator \(2^3\times5\) require when written as a terminating decimal?

Show Solution
Multiply numerator and denominator by \(5^2\) to make the denominator \(2^3\times5^3=1000\). It can be expressed with at most three decimal places.
Q13

Find rational numbers between two rational numbers by using a common denominator.

Show Solution
Convert both endpoints to equivalent fractions with a sufficiently large common denominator. Then choose numerators strictly between the endpoint numerators. The exact answer depends on the values given in the textbook question.
Q14

If \(x+y+z=0\) and \(xy+yz+zx=0\), show that \(x=y=z=0\).

Show Solution
\((x+y+z)^2=x^2+y^2+z^2+2(xy+yz+zx)\). Both given expressions are zero, so \(x^2+y^2+z^2=0\). Thus each square is zero, giving \(x=y=z=0\).
Q15

Show that the average of two distinct rational numbers lies between them.

Show Solution
If \(a<b\), then \((a+b)/2-a=(b-a)/2>0\) and \(b-(a+b)/2=(b-a)/2>0\). Therefore \(a<(a+b)/2<b\).
Q16

Find the hypotenuse lengths in a square-root spiral.

Show Solution
In the square-root spiral, each new right triangle has one leg of length 1 and the other leg equal to the previous hypotenuse. By Pythagoras: \(h_{n+1}^2=h_n^2+1\).
The sequence of hypotenuse lengths is \(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,2,\sqrt5,\sqrt6,\sqrt7,\sqrt8,3,\ldots\).

Final Revision Checklist

  • Know the difference between rational and irrational numbers.
  • Check prime factors of the denominator to classify decimal expansions.
  • Use powers of 10 and subtraction to convert repeating decimals into fractions.
  • Find rational numbers between two values using a common denominator or an average.
  • Use Pythagoras' theorem for the square-root spiral.
