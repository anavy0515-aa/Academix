Symbols and basic facts

  • O: centre of a circle; r: radius.
  • Chord: segment joining two points on the circle.
  • Diameter: chord passing through the centre; its length is 2r.
  • Circumcentre: centre of the circle through the three vertices of a triangle.
  • Cyclic quadrilateral: a quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on one circle.
  • SSS: three corresponding sides equal; SAS: two sides and their included angle equal; RHS: right angle, hypotenuse and one side equal.
A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord. This fact is used repeatedly in the numerical solutions below.

Exercise Set 5.1

Q1 — Construction and circumcentre

Show solution
Construct AB = 5 cm. At A construct a 70° angle and at B a 60° angle; the rays meet at C.
∠C = 180° − 70° − 60° = 50°.
All three angles are acute, so the circumcentre is inside the triangle.
Draw the perpendicular bisectors of two sides. Their intersection is O. Draw the circle with centre O and radius OA.

Q2 — Circumcentre of an obtuse triangle

Show solution
The specified triangle has an angle of 100°, so it is obtuse. The circumcentre of an obtuse triangle lies outside the triangle. Construct two perpendicular bisectors to locate it; then draw the circumcircle through the three vertices.

Q3 — Equal distances from the circumcentre

Show calculation
Let M be the midpoint of AB. Given CA = CB = 7 cm and AB = 6 cm, we get AM = 3 cm and CM ⟂ AB.
In right triangle CMA:
CM² = 7² − 3² = 40, so CM = 2√10 cm.
Let the circumradius be R. With O on CM, OM = 2√10 − R.
In right triangle OMA:
R² = (2√10 − R)² + 3².
Expanding and cancelling R² gives 4√10 R = 49.
Therefore OA = OB = OC = 49/(4√10) cm ≈ 3.87 cm.

Q4 — Least possible radius through two points

Show reasoning
Let M be the midpoint of AB and let the centre be at perpendicular distance h from M. Then the radius satisfies
r² = h² + (AB/2)².
Since h² ≥ 0, the radius is smallest when h = 0. The centre is then M and AB is a diameter.
Minimum radius = AB/2.

Exercise Set 5.2

Q1 — Triangle made by two radii and a chord

Show proof
Let AB be a chord and O the centre. Join OA and OB.
OA = OB because both are radii of the same circle.
A triangle with two equal sides is isosceles. Hence △OAB is isosceles.

Q2 — Congruence of two such triangles

Show proof
For triangles OAB and OCD, all four segments OA, OB, OC and OD are radii. Thus OA = OC and OB = OD. If the bases AB and CD are also equal, all three corresponding sides match.
Therefore △OAB ≅ △OCD by SSS.

Exercise Set 5.3

Q1 — Perpendicular from centre to a chord

Show proof
In right triangles OMA and OMB, OA = OB (radii) and OM is common. Both triangles have a right angle at M.
By RHS congruence, △OMA ≅ △OMB. Therefore AM = MB: the perpendicular bisects the chord.

Q2 — Altitude of an isosceles triangle

Show proof
Given AB = AC, point A is equally distant from B and C. The circle's centre O also satisfies OB = OC.
Every point equidistant from B and C lies on the perpendicular bisector of BC. Both A and O lie on it.
Therefore the perpendicular from A to BC passes through O; it is the altitude and the perpendicular bisector of BC.

Q3 — Distance between the midpoints of parallel chords

Show calculation
Radius = 5 cm. The chords are 8 cm and 6 cm long, so their half-lengths are 4 cm and 3 cm.
For the first chord, distance from O to its midpoint:
OM² = 5² − 4² = 9, so OM = 3 cm.
For the second chord:
ON² = 5² − 3² = 16, so ON = 4 cm.
The parallel chords lie on opposite sides of O, so MN = OM + ON = 7 cm.

Exercise Set 5.4

Q1 — Equal chords and equal distances

Show proof
Let AB = CD. Draw OM ⟂ AB and ON ⟂ CD. These perpendiculars bisect the chords, so AM = CN.
In right triangles OMA and ONC, OA = OC (radii). By Pythagoras:
OA² = OM² + AM²
OC² = ON² + CN².
Since OA = OC and AM = CN, it follows that OM² = ON². Distances are positive, so OM = ON.

Q2 — Chords at equal perpendicular distances

Show proof
Let CE and CH be equal perpendicular distances from centre C to chords AB and GF. The perpendiculars bisect the chords, so E and H are their midpoints.
CA = CG (radii), CE = CH, and both triangles CEA and CHG are right-angled. By RHS, AE = GH.
Hence AB = 2AE = 2GH = GF.

Q3 — Alternative proof using Pythagoras

Show proof
Since E and H are the chord midpoints, AB = 2AE and GF = 2GH.
In right triangles CEA and CHG:
CA² = CE² + AE²
CG² = CH² + GH².
CA = CG and CE = CH, so AE² = GH². Since lengths are positive, AE = GH.
Therefore AB = GF.

Exercise Set 5.5

Q1 — Chord length for radius 7 cm and distance 6 cm

Show calculation
Let half the chord be x. The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord.
7² = 6² + x²
49 = 36 + x²
x² = 13, so x = √13 cm.
Full chord = 2x = 2√13 cm ≈ 7.21 cm.

Q2 — General chord-length formula

Show derivation
Let the radius be r, perpendicular distance from centre to chord be d, and half-chord length be x.
By Pythagoras, r² = d² + x².
Therefore x = √(r² − d²).
The full chord is twice its half-length:
Chord length = 2√(r² − d²).

Q3 — Does doubling the distance halve/double the chord?

Show reasoning
No. Chord length is not directly proportional to its distance from the centre. It depends on the expression 2√(r² − d²).
For example, choose radius 5 cm. A chord 2 cm from the centre has length 2√21 cm. A chord 4 cm from the centre has length 6 cm.
The second chord is not half or double the first. Thus doubling the perpendicular distance does not establish a fixed ratio between chord lengths.

Exercise Set 5.6

Q1 — Chord subtending 60° at the centre

Show calculation
OA = OB = 12 cm, since both are radii. In △OAB, the angle at O is 60° and the other two angles are equal.
Each remaining angle is (180° − 60°)/2 = 60°.
The triangle is equilateral, so AB = 12 cm.

Q2(i) — Angles subtended by a chord on the same side

Show reasoning
Two points X and Y on the same side of chord AB on the circumference see the same arc AB. The angle subtended by that arc at the centre is twice either angle on the circumference.
Therefore ∠AXB = ∠AYB.

Q2(ii) — Points on opposite sides of the chord

Show reasoning
Not always. If AB is a diameter, every point on the circle subtends a right angle on AB. So X and Y may lie on opposite sides and still have equal angles of 90°.
For a non-diameter chord, equal inscribed angles correspond to points on the same side of the chord.

Q2(iii) — Whether a circle through A, B and X must contain Y

Show reasoning
Not necessarily. Equal angles alone do not establish concyclicity if X and Y are on opposite sides of AB. The converse theorem applies when the two points lie on the same side of AB and subtend equal angles to AB.
In that stated configuration, the circle through A, B and X also passes through Y.

Q3 — Angle in a cyclic quadrilateral

Show calculation
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
x + 100° = 180°
x = 80°.

The 12 theorems — statements and easy proofs

Use these as a theorem revision sheet. The diagrams below are original schematic drawings, not scans of the textbook figures.

Theorem 1 — Unique circle through three non-collinear points

Proof idea Three non-collinear points A, B and C determine perpendicular bisectors of AB and AC. These meet at one point O. Since O is equally far from A, B and C, the circle centred at O through A passes through all three. The centre and radius are fixed, so the circle is unique.

Theorem 2 — Equal chords give equal central angles

Proof OA = OC and OB = OD (radii), while AB = CD (given). Thus △OAB ≅ △OCD by SSS. Corresponding angles are equal, so ∠AOB = ∠COD.

Theorem 3 — Equal central angles give equal chords

Proof OA = OC and OB = OD (radii), and the included angles are equal. By SAS, △OAB ≅ △OCD. Therefore AB = CD.

Theorem 4 — Centre joined to chord midpoint is perpendicular

Proof Let M be the midpoint of AB. OA = OB, AM = MB and OM is common. Thus △OMA ≅ △OMB by SSS. Their angles at M are equal and form a straight angle, so each is 90°. Hence OM ⟂ AB.

Theorem 5 — Perpendicular from centre bisects the chord

Proof If OM ⟂ AB, triangles OMA and OMB are right triangles. OA = OB (radii) and OM is common. RHS congruence gives AM = MB.

Theorem 6 — Equal chords are equally distant from the centre

Proof Draw perpendiculars from O to equal chords AB and CD. Each perpendicular bisects its chord. Their half-chords are equal; the radii are equal too. Pythagoras in the two right triangles gives equal perpendicular distances.

Theorem 7 — Chords equally distant from the centre are equal

Proof The two right triangles have equal hypotenuse (radii) and equal perpendicular side (distance from centre). By RHS congruence, their half-chords are equal. Doubling both gives equal full chords.

Theorem 8 — Longer chord is nearer the centre

Proof For radius r and perpendicular distance d, chord length is 2√(r² − d²). As d increases, r² − d² decreases, so chord length decreases. Thus the longer chord is nearer the centre.

Theorem 9 — Central angle is double the inscribed angle

Proof idea Join the centre to the endpoints of the arc and to the point on the circumference. Radii form isosceles triangles. Using the triangle angle sum and the exterior-angle property gives the central angle as twice the angle at the circumference.

Corollary — Angle in a semicircle is 90°

Proof A diameter subtends 180° at the centre. The angle at the circumference on the same arc is half of 180°, which is 90°.

Theorem 10 — Equal angles can prove four points concyclic

Proof idea Draw the unique circle through A, B and C. If D lies on the same side of AB as C and ∠ADB = ∠ACB, the converse of the inscribed-angle theorem places D on that same circle. Therefore A, B, C and D are concyclic.

Theorem 11 — Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral

Proof Each inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc. The two arcs corresponding to opposite angles together make a full turn of 360°. Their half-sum is 180°. Hence opposite angles add to 180°.

Theorem 12 — Converse cyclic quadrilateral theorem

Proof idea If one pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral sums to 180°, draw the circle through three vertices. The supplementary-angle condition forces the fourth vertex to lie on that circle. Therefore the quadrilateral is cyclic.

End-of-chapter exercises — solution notes

These are worked notes keyed by number; question statements are omitted. Refer to the same numbered problems in your textbook.

Q1

Show solution
Let half the chord be x. With radius 13 cm and perpendicular distance 5 cm:
13² = 5² + x²
x² = 169 − 25 = 144, so x = 12 cm.
Full chord = 24 cm.

Q2

Show solution
The angle on the circumference is half the central angle for the same arc.
70° ÷ 2 = 35°.

Q3

Show solution
Radius = 26/2 = 13 cm. Half-chord = 24/2 = 12 cm.
d² = 13² − 12² = 169 − 144 = 25.
d = 5 cm.

Q4

Show solution
Let half-chord be x. Then 15² = 9² + x².
x² = 225 − 81 = 144, so x = 12 cm.
Full chord = 24 cm.

Q5

Show proof
Let M be the midpoint of chord AB and O the centre. OA = OB (radii), AM = MB (midpoint) and OM is common. Thus △OMA ≅ △OMB by SSS. The angles at M are equal and together form 180°, so both are 90°. Therefore OM is perpendicular to AB and the perpendicular bisector passes through O.

Q6

Show proof
A diameter subtends 180° at the centre. The angle subtended at any other point on the circumference is half the central angle.
∠ACB = 180°/2 = 90°.

Q7

Show calculation
Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.
∠C = 180° − 75° = 105°.
∠D = 180° − 110° = 70°.

Q8

Show calculation
(2x + 10) + (3x − 20) = 180°
5x − 10 = 180
5x = 190, so x = 38.
∠P = 2(38) + 10 = 86°.
∠R = 3(38) − 20 = 94°.

Q9

Show calculation
Half of the 16 cm chord is 8 cm. The radius, half-chord and perpendicular distance form a right triangle.
r² = 8² + 6² = 64 + 36 = 100.
r = 10 cm.

Q10

Show solution
For a cyclic quadrilateral with side lengths 5, 5, 12 and 12, a diagonal splits it into two congruent 5–12–13 right triangles in the adjacent-equal-side arrangement. Each triangle has area (1/2) × 5 × 12 = 30.
Total area = 30 + 30 = 60 square units.

Q11

Show method
Draw a diagonal AC. The circumcircle centre is also the circumcentre of triangles ABC and ACD. Determine whether these triangles are acute, right or obtuse using their angles. An acute triangle has its circumcentre inside; a right triangle has it at the midpoint of its hypotenuse; an obtuse triangle has it outside. This locates the centre relative to the quadrilateral.

Q12

Show proof idea
Let two equal chords AB and CD intersect at P. Draw perpendiculars from O to the chords, meeting them at M and N. Since the chords are equal, OM = ON and their half-lengths are equal. Right triangles OMP and ONP then give MP = NP by Pythagoras. Consequently the longer pieces on the two chords match, and the shorter pieces match.

Q13

Show construction
Draw AB = 6 cm. At B construct BC perpendicular to AB and mark BC = 6 cm. Join AC. The triangle is right-angled at B, so its circumcentre is the midpoint of hypotenuse AC. Draw the circle centred there through A, B and C.
AC = √(6² + 6²) = 6√2, so radius = 3√2 cm.
The perpendicular distance from centre to AB is 3 cm.

Q14

Show proof
In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal. In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles sum to 180°. Thus if ∠A = ∠C and ∠A + ∠C = 180°, then each is 90°. Similarly the other two angles are right angles. Hence the parallelogram is a rectangle. Conversely, every rectangle is cyclic because its opposite angles sum to 180°.

Q15

Show proof
In a rectangle inscribed in a circle, each interior angle is 90°. The angle in a semicircle theorem implies each diagonal subtends a right angle at the circumference and is therefore a diameter. Both diagonals pass through the circle's centre, so their intersection is the centre.

Q16

Show reasoning
For a fixed chord length L in a circle of radius r, its midpoint is at distance d = √(r² − (L/2)²) from the centre. This distance is the same for every chord of that fixed length. The locus of points at fixed distance d from the centre is a circle. Therefore the midpoints form a concentric circle of radius √(r² − (L/2)²).

Q17

Show proof
Given AB = AC, OA is common, and OB = OC because both are radii. Thus △OAB ≅ △OAC by SSS. Corresponding angles ∠OAB and ∠OAC are equal, so AO bisects ∠BAC. Hence the centre lies on the angle bisector.

Q18

Show calculation
Let the perpendicular distances from the centre to the 10 cm and 24 cm chords be d₁ and d₂. Their half-lengths are 5 cm and 12 cm. The longer chord is closer to the centre, and the gap between them is 7 cm, so d₁ = d₂ + 7.
Since both radii are the same:
(d₂ + 7)² + 5² = d₂² + 12².
14d₂ + 74 = 144, so d₂ = 5 cm and d₁ = 12 cm.
r² = 5² + 12² = 169, so r = 13 cm.

Q19

Show proof
The six equal sides of a regular hexagon subtend six equal central angles. Each is 360°/6 = 60°. A triangle formed by two radii and one side therefore has all three angles equal to 60° and is equilateral.
Each side of the hexagon equals r.
The perpendicular distance from the centre to a side is √(r² − (r/2)²) = (√3/2)r.

Q20

Show reasoning
The two stated angles stand on the same chord MP from the same segment of the circle. Therefore the angles are equal by the same-segment theorem: ∠MOP = ∠MNP (O here is a vertex of the quadrilateral, not the centre).

Q21

Show proof
At a vertex of a cyclic quadrilateral, the exterior angle and its adjacent interior angle add to 180°. The opposite interior angle and that same interior angle also add to 180°. Since both sums are 180°, the exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle.

Q22

Show proof
For any chord AB, triangle OAB has OA = OB = r. By the triangle inequality, AB ≤ OA + OB = 2r. Since 2r is the diameter, no chord is longer than the diameter. Equality holds only when AB passes through O.

Q23

Show proof idea
For any chord through an interior point A, let M be its midpoint. The perpendicular OM to the chord gives chord length 2√(r² − OM²). Since OA is fixed and OM ≤ OA, the chord is shortest when OM is largest, namely OM = OA. This happens when M = A, so the chord is perpendicular to OA at A.

Q24

Show proof
Join the centre O to the point A on the circumference of the semicircle. OA and the two half-diameters are radii, so the two smaller triangles are isosceles. If their base angles are a and b, the full angle at A is a + b. The triangle's angles give (a + b) + (a + b) = 180°, so a + b = 90°. Thus the angle in a semicircle is 90°.

Q25

Show proof
Reflection across diameter AB maps the circle onto itself. Since the two stated chords are perpendicular to AB, their endpoints are exchanged by this reflection. The midpoint M of one chord is mapped to the midpoint M′ of the other. A point and its reflected image are joined by a segment perpendicular to the mirror line. Hence MM′ ⟂ AB.

Q26

Show proof
Join the centre to all four vertices of the cyclic quadrilateral. The four triangles formed are isosceles because each pair of sides from the centre are radii.
Let the equal base-angle parts be p, q, u and v. Then the opposite-angle sums are both p + q + u + v, so ∠A + ∠C = ∠B + ∠D.
All four interior angles sum to 360°. Since the two opposite-angle sums are equal, each is 180°.
Therefore ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°.

Quick revision

  • Diameter = 2r.
  • Chord length = 2√(r² − d²).
  • Equal chords ↔ equal central angles.
  • Equal chords ↔ equal perpendicular distances from centre.
  • Longer chord lies closer to the centre.
  • Central angle = 2 × angle at circumference on same arc.
  • Angle in a semicircle = 90°.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
